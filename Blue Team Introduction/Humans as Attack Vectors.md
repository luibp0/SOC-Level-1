# Humans as Attack Vectors

## Overview

This room focuses on how attackers target people rather than systems directly. It introduces social engineering techniques and explains how human behavior can be exploited to gain access to sensitive information or systems.

## Topics Covered

- Human-Based Attacks
- Social Engineering
- Phishing
- Spear Phishing
- Pretexting
- Baiting
- Tailgating
- Impersonation
- Credential Theft
- Security Awareness

## Common Attack Techniques

- **Phishing:** Sending malicious messages to trick users into revealing information or clicking malicious links.
- **Spear Phishing:** Targeting a specific individual or organization with a customized phishing attack.
- **Pretexting:** Creating a fake scenario to convince a victim to provide sensitive information.
- **Baiting:** Using an attractive offer or item to trick a victim.
- **Tailgating:** Gaining unauthorized physical access by following an authorized person.
- **Impersonation:** Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.

## Security Measures

Organizations can reduce human-related security risks through:

- Security awareness training
- Strong authentication and MFA
- Phishing awareness
- Verification procedures
- Reporting suspicious activity
- Following security policies

## Skills Learned

- Recognizing common social engineering attacks
- Identifying phishing attempts
- Understanding how attackers manipulate human behavior
- Understanding the importance of security awareness
- Applying basic defensive measures against human-based attacks

## Key Takeaway

People can be targeted as an entry point into an organization. Security awareness, verification, and proper security procedures are essential for reducing the risk of social engineering attacks.
