# SOC L1 Alert Reporting

## Overview

This room focuses on how Level 1 SOC Analysts document and report security alerts. It explains how clear and accurate reporting helps other analysts understand what happened, what was investigated, and what actions were taken.

## Topics Covered

- Alert Reporting
- Incident Documentation
- Investigation Notes
- Evidence Collection
- Alert Classification
- Reporting Structure
- Escalation Details
- Analyst Communication
- Recommendations

## Alert Reporting Process

- **Review the Alert:** Understand the security event and its context.
- **Document the Evidence:** Record relevant information such as IP addresses, usernames, timestamps, domains, and affected systems.
- **Describe the Investigation:** Explain the steps taken during the analysis.
- **Record Findings:** Clearly state whether the activity is legitimate or suspicious.
- **Document Actions:** Record any containment or response actions performed.
- **Escalate if Necessary:** Provide enough information for higher-level analysts to continue the investigation.

## Important Report Information

A good SOC alert report may include:

- Alert name and description
- Date and time
- Source and destination information
- Affected user or system
- Indicators of Compromise (IoCs)
- Investigation steps
- Evidence and findings
- Alert classification and severity
- Actions taken
- Recommended next steps

## SOC L1 Responsibilities

A Level 1 SOC Analyst should:

- Keep investigation notes clear and accurate
- Include relevant evidence
- Avoid unnecessary information
- Document actions taken
- Provide context for escalation
- Ensure another analyst can understand and continue the investigation

## Skills Learned

- Writing clear security alert reports
- Documenting investigation findings
- Recording relevant evidence
- Identifying important alert information
- Communicating findings with other SOC analysts
- Preparing alerts for escalation

## Key Takeaway

Good alert reporting ensures that security investigations are properly documented and that important information can be quickly understood by other SOC team members.
